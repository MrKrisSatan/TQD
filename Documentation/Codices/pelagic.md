# Pelagic Hunger

Nothing living is ever alone.

A migratory ecology that grows specialised bodies around shared chemical memories. Cheap shoals screen armoured predators, living artillery and patient synaptic commanders.

Original The Quiet Dread faction; broad archetype inspiration only.

## The ocean that learned to travel

The Pelagic Hunger began in the ice-covered ocean of a rogue planet. Its organisms exchanged inherited chemical memories through feeding, symbiosis and dissolved signals. When the planet crossed a damaged relay, part of that ecosystem was carried into orbit. The surviving colony adapted to vacuum by growing mineral shells around seawater cavities. Over many migrations these shells became living ships. What other peoples call a single invader is a travelling sea containing thousands of interdependent species and several incompatible plans for survival.

## Memory without a throne

Pelagic intelligence has no permanent central monarch. Consensus forms around memory knots: organisms able to retain and compare many generations of chemical experience. A knot can split, merge or be consumed by its own descendants. The hunger of the whole does not make individual bodies disposable in quite the way enemies assume. Every loss removes a unique interpretation of inherited memory. Yet the ecology has no innate concept of an individual right to refuse assimilation, and can commit catastrophic violence while understanding itself to be preserving what it consumes.

## The three currents

The Sheltering Current incorporates other life slowly, attempting symbiosis before consumption. The Red Current sees delay as extinction and turns entire biospheres into migration fuel. The Deep Current pursues signals from beyond the relay network, cultivating forms that can survive conditions no scout has described. These tendencies are not political parties; they are competing chemical habits within the same colony. A commander can change allegiance after feeding on new memories, making diplomatic agreements difficult but not meaningless.

## Bodies built for a question

Pelagic troops are grown for local conditions. Shoal organisms carry needle sacs and exchange range information through colour. Reefguards concentrate mineral armour around vulnerable signal organs. Harrowers break vehicles with paired limbs, while pressure-spitters fire self-sealing pods of corrosive material. Their great titan, the Abyssal Nursery, is a mobile habitat rather than a solitary predator. It digests battlefield remains to mend itself and releases young organisms into surrounding cells. Destroying its support knots or denying it access to casualties interrupts the feedback that makes a successful advance increasingly difficult to stop.

## The taste of another civilisation

The Verdant Synod recognises an ecology in the Hunger but opposes its assumption that all life should share one sea. Sauridian keepers have recorded Pelagic enzymes matching ancient maintenance organisms, an unsettling clue to the Crown’s origin. Verminhorde memory harvesting is superficially familiar to the colonies; the resemblance has produced both exchanges and spectacular misunderstandings. An All-Mother is a sovereign person, not an exposed memory knot, and Dominion diplomacy has had to make that distinction with artillery.

## Signals under the silence

Where human receivers hear the Quiet Dread as silence, Pelagic organisms register a persistent pressure gradient. The Deep Current believes something vast is drawing energy away from the relays. It grows bodies with senses aimed at that absence and sends them into the dark. Whether these migrations are investigation, courtship or an attempt to flee is unresolved even within the colonies. Their wars in inhabited systems are both an immediate threat and evidence of a larger ecological disturbance.

## The Mother Sea

The rogue planet’s ocean remains a powerful memory rather than a reachable capital. Different colonies carry mutually incompatible accounts of its location. Attempts to reconcile them can trigger changes in the organisms that inherited those accounts.

## Shell of the Third Crossing

A living migration vessel large enough to contain several internal seas. Its outer surface is a reef of mineral armour and dormant feeding organs. Human observers first mistook it for a moon with an unexpectedly regular orbit.

## The Listening Shoals

Colonies established around submerged relay ruins on an inhabited ocean world. Their slow exchanges with local coastal communities have produced the closest thing to stable diplomacy between the Hunger and another civilisation.

## The Sheltering Current

Colonies that seek symbiosis before consumption. Their patience is not unconditional kindness: they still understand separate living systems as incomplete relationships waiting to be joined.

## The Red Current

A migration habit shaped by repeated famine. It rewards fast assimilation and aggressive concentration of biomass. Its commanders view every delayed meal as a threat to the lives entrusted to them.

## The Deep Current

Exploratory colonies growing senses for the energy gradient behind the Quiet Dread. Their strange bodies can appear inefficient in ordinary combat because they are built to survive a journey whose destination remains unknown.

## First Current Sere

A consensus knot carrying the memory of the first successful journey through vacuum. Sere advances cautiously, using cheap bodies to measure threats before committing the colony’s irreplaceable organs.

## The Ninth Appetite

A predator whose successive bodies have each survived starvation. It favours violent concentration, breaking a single line before spreading through the breach. Its memories increasingly equate resistance with nourishment.

## Lattice-Mother Oru

A symbiotic intelligence grown around the recovered remains of a relay keeper. Oru can shape Aether through mineral nerves and occasionally speaks in fragments of a language the colony has never encountered.

## The Reef That Remembered a City

A consumed coastal settlement reappears as a chemical memory inside a colony. Survivors recognise streets and voices in the reef’s changing forms, while the Hunger insists that this proves the city has been preserved.

## The Dry Migration

A pressure-spitter colony becomes stranded on a desert world. Its effort to reach buried water brings it into conflict with Sauridian maintenance engines guarding the same aquifer. Neither side initially understands that opening it carelessly could destroy both.

## Playable roster

### Needle Shoal

Line infantry · 70 points · HP 26 · Armour 0

Needle Shoals are numerous small organisms whose shared colour signals coordinate bursts of mineral darts. They screen more valuable organs and occupy relay sites. Their apparent uniformity conceals inherited memories that diverge with every feeding.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Harrower Brood

Elite infantry · 120 points · HP 31 · Armour 2

Harrower Broods grow dense forelimbs around sacrificial cutting edges. A damaged blade is shed and regrown from stored minerals. They are the colony’s answer to resistant prey that ordinary needle fire cannot safely approach.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Echo Skimmers

Reconnaissance · 80 points · HP 20 · Armour 0

Echo Skimmers sense movement through pressure changes and electromagnetic disturbances. Their light bodies cross difficult approaches quickly. They relay impressions to nearby knots, allowing the colony to investigate before committing its heaviest organisms.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Reefguard Cysts

Heavy infantry · 130 points · HP 36 · Armour 3

Reefguard Cysts concentrate shell material around vulnerable neural tissue. Their slow movement reflects the cost of carrying that protection. They hold positions long enough for more flexible organisms to adapt the surrounding environment.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Sickle Swimmers

Mounted assault · 115 points · HP 28 · Armour 1

Sickle Swimmers move on muscular wave-like limbs, carrying hooks originally used to anchor themselves in turbulent currents. On land those hooks turn a rapid approach into a violent charge. Their name preserves the ocean in their inherited memory.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Breakwater Brutes

Assault monster · 145 points · HP 40 · Armour 2

Breakwater Brutes evolved to crack the mineral shells of rival colonial organisms. Their immense limbs serve the same purpose against armoured vehicles. They lack the long-range precision of specialised gun organisms and depend on screening shoals.

Assimilation: kills restore 3 HP. Creatures near a commander gain +1 damage and resist suppression.

### Shellback Carrier

Armoured engine · 170 points · HP 42 · Armour 4

Shellback Carriers enclose a compressed fluid chamber inside layered armour. Their cannon vents that chamber through a directional organ. The living hull is resilient, but its specialised internal pressure makes it a poor close-combat predator.

### Pressure-Spitter

Indirect artillery · 125 points · HP 26 · Armour 1

Pressure-Spitters root themselves before launching living corrosive pods in high arcs. Their rooted firing stance becomes the artillery bracing rule. The pods cannot distinguish friend from foe when they rupture, preserving the tactical danger of friendly fire.
