# Verdant Synod

A world is not a resource. It is a witness.

Forest peoples, rangers and sentient ecosystems share a living military network of guns, spores and spirits.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## The forest that learned to answer

The Verdant Synod arose when Aether currents connected ancient forests, engineered reclamation ecosystems and the people living within them. Some landscapes acquired distributed awareness; others revealed minds that had always existed but lacked a language their neighbours could hear. The first recognised Synod was an agreement between a river basin, three ranger communities and a damaged weather-control network.

This origin makes the faction neither a simple return to nature nor an enemy of all technology. Many of its sacred groves contain deliberate bioengineering, and some treefolk trace their lineage to industrial remediation projects. The Synod judges tools by their place in a living system. A rifle grown from bioceramic tissue is acceptable if its production and use can be sustained. A beautiful garden maintained by draining another world is not innocent merely because its owners prefer leaves to smokestacks.

## Many minds, one watershed

A Synod combines local peoples with the landscapes that sustain them. Rangers, treefolk, human settlers and other inhabitants choose Speakers who interpret between individual and distributed minds. Decisions may take hours or decades depending on whether the question concerns a military patrol or the future course of a river. Emergency commanders are therefore granted temporary authority bounded by explicit ecological obligations.

The system can fail through unequal representation. A vast old forest may consider a century a short delay while a village needs medicine tomorrow. Speakers can exploit the difficulty of verifying what a landscape truly said. The Synod trains independent listeners to challenge such claims. Its most important civil right is the right to dissent from communion: joining a shared awareness must not erase the individual or make privacy appear to be treason against the ecosystem.

## A war fought through the senses

Rootwatch Rangers and Leafglass Stalkers favour accurate ranged fire, concealed movement and target denial. Thornbound Wardens defend chokepoints, while Briarhart Riders exploit routes heavy machines cannot use efficiently. Groveheart Colossi provide durable fire support at the cost of slow replacement. Sporefall artillery attacks from behind cover, and Cantors coordinate magic through living networks.

Living Veil gives infantry persistent cover and additional movement when starting in physical cover. The force can evade and endure shooting better than its thin armour suggests, but close assault and cover-ignoring weapons remain dangerous. Biological logistics are not effortless: living ammunition needs nutrients, colossi need recovery time and a damaged ecosystem cannot simply be ordered to increase production. The Synod’s commanders must decide how much of the world they can consume while claiming to defend it.

## The boundaries of restoration

The Synod trades food and medicines with the Compact and negotiates migration routes with the Hornbound. It fights the Kharad over destructive mining yet buys carefully limited tools from their most scrupulous guilds. Sauridian solar engineering can restore damaged climates, but the Dynasty sometimes assumes that old stewardship grants present ownership.

The Dominion represents a concealed ecological threat because a surface forest can appear healthy while its water systems are being diverted below. The Hollow Star corrupts the meaning of communion by turning shared feeling into compulsory devotion. The Synod’s own extremists can commit a related error: treating the restoration of an ideal landscape as permission to remove the people currently living there. The best Speakers insist that a world’s memory includes its cities, scars and newcomers, not only the version they find easiest to love.

## Everdusk Basin

A continent-sized watershed whose distributed mind changes emphasis with the seasons. Its capital is a moving assembly rather than a permanent throne.

## The Glassleaf Reaches

Forests adapted to industrial contaminants now produce useful optical fibres. Their existence complicates any claim that healing means restoring an untouched past.

## Seven Springs

A world whose seven surviving water systems each elect a Speaker through different customs. Thalen’s people preserve the memory of a failed attempt to merge them by force.

## Rootwatch Communes

Settled ranger communities favour patient defence and negotiated ecological limits. They are skilled at preserving territory but sometimes reluctant to aid distant landscapes.

## The Leafglass Hunt

Mobile scouts and marksmen disrupt enemy logistics. Their doctrine avoids unnecessary destruction, though isolated cells can become unaccountable judges of what is necessary.

## The Deep Communion

Treefolk and Cantors cultivate large-scale shared awareness. Their political challenge is respecting short-lived people whose needs cannot wait for a forest’s deliberation.

## The Cinderseed Covenant

Restoration specialists work in ruined industrial worlds rather than pristine groves. Traditionalists call their hybrid ecosystems impure; survivors call them habitable.

## Speaker Naeva Rootsong

Naeva grew up in a mining settlement blamed for poisoning the forest beside it. She uncovered evidence that an upstream concession had diverted the treatment funds and persuaded both communities to defend the evidence together. Chosen as Speaker by people and forest alike, she treats translation as a duty to reveal disagreement, not conceal it. Her armies coordinate rifle fire and patient territorial defence.

## Elder Thalen of Seven Springs

Thalen participated in an early Synod campaign that redirected a river to restore one sacred grove and destroyed several downstream communities. He has spent centuries opposing the belief that ecological scale makes individual suffering irrelevant. His great body is a formidable war engine, but his counsel is cautious. He remembers that an army can sincerely protect life while making terrible decisions about which lives count.

## Hunt-Marshal Sira Leafglass

Sira commands dispersed ranger cells through signals too quiet for most machines to distinguish from weather. She prefers to disable depots and transport routes rather than attack settlements. Her restraint is strategic as well as ethical: a civilian who can live after the war is less likely to become the next enemy. Her hunters have repeatedly exposed Dominion water theft beneath apparently untouched forests.

## 33 AC — The First Answering

A damaged river-control network began speaking through nearby roots. Its negotiated repair became the founding agreement of the modern Synod.

## 156 AC — The Misguided Spring

An attempt to restore one ecosystem by force ruined another. The resulting inquiry established independent listeners and a formal right to refuse communion.

## 304 AC — The Glassleaf Recovery

Rangers and industrial refugees rebuilt a poisoned region into a stable hybrid forest. The campaign remains a political argument about whether recovery must resemble the past.
