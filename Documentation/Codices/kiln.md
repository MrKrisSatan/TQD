# Kilnclaw Assemblage

Build it loud. Own the dawn.

Greenskin foundry clans who elect war leaders by completing impossible machines. Dense gun mobs, ram engines and volatile siege artillery turn scrap into an industrial migration.

Original The Quiet Dread faction; broad archetype inspiration only.

## The factories that walked away

Kilnclaw history begins with labour colonies abandoned when their freight routes failed. The inhabitants dismantled their factories, mounted the furnaces on salvaged traction frames and followed the remaining rails toward places with fuel. Generations later, the moving foundries had become cities and their maintenance crews had become clans. The Assemblage is not a single nation. It is a temporary agreement between engines travelling in roughly the same direction, maintained by bargaining, contests and the shared knowledge that every furnace eventually needs someone else’s spare parts.

## A title earned in public

A Kilnclaw chief must complete a public work before claiming command: bridge a ravine, restart a dead reactor or defeat a rival machine without destroying the salvage. Strength matters, but a strong leader who cannot keep the wheels turning soon loses followers. Smaller greenskins serve as gaugesmiths, ammunition runners and negotiators. They are often exploited, yet their control of measurements gives them leverage that outsiders miss. A chief may ignore an adviser; ignoring the only person who knows the boiler’s safe pressure is another matter.

## The assembly and the argument

Clans gather at great circles of parked engines to settle routes and allocate salvage. Decisions are shouted, wagered and occasionally fought over, but a binding agreement is stamped into metal and displayed on every participating furnace. The Assemblage’s wars begin when no route satisfies its appetite for ore and fuel. Some chiefs bargain for access; others declare the inhabitants of a rich world to be obstacles invented by idle people. Kilnclaw culture contains generosity toward useful strangers and an alarming indifference toward anyone it cannot recognise as a contributor.

## The sound of industry

Gun mobs fight around armoured mobile workshops. Their weapons are crude in finish but often ingenious in mechanism, designed to accept several kinds of salvaged ammunition. Ram carts open lanes for weldguard infantry; boiler brutes carry enough pressure to power both limbs and close-range guns. The Marching Kiln, a towering furnace on enormous feet, converts a kill into a surge of ammunition and momentum. Its overload attack fires an extra barrage but damages its own hull. Patient opponents can bait the chief into burning through the engine before its assault reaches their line.

## Rivals in the same green

Cinderjaw Freeholds view the Assemblage as noisy upstarts who have mistaken machinery for courage. Kilnclaw chiefs call the Freeholds landlords with axes. Their shared ancestry does not prevent bitter conflicts over salvage routes, and their battlefield habits differ: Freehold warbands emphasise massed charges, while Assemblage armies drag heavy industry into the firing line. Vault engineers despise their tolerances but sometimes secretly purchase Kilnclaw solutions to problems too irregular for a formal specification.

## When the furnaces go quiet

The first sign of the Quiet Dread in Assemblage territory was a row of furnaces that continued to burn without making a sound. Workers could feel the pistons but could no longer hear their own tools. The affected engines were abandoned after their pressure gauges began rising with no increase in fuel. Some chiefs believe these silent furnaces offer limitless power; others insist that a machine which cannot be heard cannot be trusted. Both factions are now sending armed expeditions toward the source.

## The Nine-Boiler Road

A moving city whose districts are connected by retractable bridges. Its route is voted on whenever the leading engine exhausts its fuel. Residents identify their homes by the boiler that currently supplies them rather than a fixed address.

## Rivetwake

A scrapyard moon surrounded by a cloud of salvage platforms. Its clans have built an entire economy around identifying which apparently useless components still contain something another machine needs.

## The Quiet Furnaces

An abandoned industrial belt where engines continue working without producing sound. Some clans treat it as a source of limitless power; others leave warning plates stating that the pressure cannot be measured honestly.

## The Nine-Boiler Compact

Foreman Brakka’s coalition of mobile foundries. Its members supply disciplined gun mobs and unusually reliable heavy vehicles because their authority depends on meeting shared maintenance promises.

## The Rivetmaw Routebreakers

Fast assault crews who win access to new salvage grounds by breaching obstacles in public. Their machines carry redundant rams so that a failed first impact does not require a humiliating retreat.

## The Sootglass Gauge

Small technicians and Aether readers whose measurements keep the great engines alive. They rarely claim formal command but can halt an army by refusing to certify its pressure.

## High Foreman Brakka Nine-Boilers

Elected after carrying a city over a salt plain on nine mismatched engines. Brakka rewards initiative and treats ammunition schedules as sacred promises. His gun mobs advance behind a moving wall of spare armour.

## Ram-Queen Uzza Rivetmaw

A celebrated bridge breaker who wins route disputes through spectacular charges. Uzza measures authority by what a leader risks personally and insists that her command platform be the first machine across a contested crossing.

## Gauge-Sage Tikk Sootglass

A small, meticulous pressure reader whose predictions resemble magic closely enough to unsettle his patrons. Tikk channels volatile Aether through copper gauges, correcting disasters one fraction of a second before they occur.

## The Bridge With Two Owners

A Vault contract and a Kilnclaw stamped agreement both claim the same abandoned transit bridge. Each side can prove its own document authentic. Their armies arrive before either has discovered that the bridge’s supports are moving.

## The Silent Shift

An entire work crew returns from the Quiet Furnaces with perfect production records and no memory of the shift. Tikk Sootglass insists the records describe an engine that has not yet been built. Rival chiefs race to assemble it before the warning can be understood.

## Playable roster

### Rivet Gunmob

Line infantry · 80 points · HP 29 · Armour 1

Rivet Gunmobs carry robust weapons adapted from industrial fastener launchers. They are less accurate than elite human files but compensate with tough bodies and forceful fire. Chiefs keep them close enough to receive both orders and replacement plates.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Weldguard Mob

Elite infantry · 130 points · HP 34 · Armour 3

Weldguard Mobs protect working engineers in the middle of an assault. Their heavy guns are paired with tools capable of cutting seized machinery free. They judge a victory partly by how much usable equipment remains afterwards.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Soot Runners

Reconnaissance · 90 points · HP 23 · Armour 1

Soot Runners scout routes and inspect salvage before the foundries commit to a march. Their lightly protected teams prefer useful information to a fair fight. The longest-range guns are often assembled from several incompatible but carefully measured parts.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Boilerplate Guard

Heavy infantry · 140 points · HP 39 · Armour 4

Boilerplate Guards wear overlapping sections of retired pressure vessels. The armour is heavy, hot and remarkably resilient. Their job is to remain in the breach while crews behind them convert captured territory into a working route.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Ram Carts

Mounted assault · 125 points · HP 31 · Armour 2

Ram Carts are compact racing engines with reinforced fronts and almost no concern for comfort. Their crews approach combat as a public test of workmanship. A successful charge advertises the chief who funded the machine.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Piston Brutes

Assault monster · 155 points · HP 43 · Armour 3

Piston Brutes strap industrial actuators to already powerful bodies. The resulting strength can tear open armour, provided the gaugesmith has kept every joint supplied with pressure. They are feared in melee and dependent on a functioning supply line.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Scrap Hauler

Armoured engine · 180 points · HP 45 · Armour 5

Scrap Haulers combine cargo capacity with a large turret gun. The military formation represents the armed machines at the head of a foundry convoy. Their armour protects valuable tools as much as the crew.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Furnace Lobber

Indirect artillery · 135 points · HP 29 · Armour 2

Furnace Lobbers fire heavy charges made from whatever the clan can safely seal inside a shell. Their trajectories remain predictable enough to require proper deployment and minimum range. Nearby allies are not protected from the resulting blast.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

### Slag Throwers

Close assault · 100 points · HP 28 · Armour 2

Slag Throwers use industrial waste as short-range ammunition. The material floods trenches and exposed firing positions, defeating cover rather than armour at a distance. Their crews must advance under protection to make the weapon useful.

Boiler rush: moved attacks gain +3 melee damage; each kill adds an extra Aether. Armour is strongest around commanders.

Gauge Sages read physical pressure and Aether resonance through the same collection of instruments. Their apparent improvisation depends on years of careful measurement. A spectacular spell is usually preceded by a very quiet correction to a valve.
