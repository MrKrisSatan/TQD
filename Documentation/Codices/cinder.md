# Cinderjaw Freeholds

Nothing is free until we have broken its chains.

Orc furnace clans and goblin convoy leagues turn stolen industry into loud, reckless liberation wars.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## Children of the furnace

The Cinderjaw Freeholds descend from orc industrial workers and goblin technical communities once bound to the Crown’s least habitable forge worlds. Their ancestors were engineered for heat tolerance, endurance and work in hazardous spaces, then dismissed as crude because the empires employing them had no interest in their language. When the relays failed, management fleets evacuated and left the workers inside sealed production zones. The first Freehold was formed by breaking an airlock from the inside.

Their liberation was neither orderly nor universal. Some revolutionary captains became the new overseers, and some clans still confuse freedom with taking whatever their engines can carry. The word Freehold therefore describes a claim constantly tested in practice. A leader must provide food, movement and a share of useful spoils. A warboss who merely orders sacrifice will eventually find the mechanics have removed something indispensable from the command wagon.

## Orcs, goblins and the share-fire

Orcs usually dominate heavy combat because their bodies favour it; goblins disproportionately command maintenance, surveying and communications. Neither pattern is a legal ceiling. A goblin who keeps twenty clans supplied can exercise more real power than an orc champion. Share-fires distribute resources publicly, with clan witnesses counting what enters the common stock. These meetings are raucous, competitive and vulnerable to intimidation, but they are not mindless disorder.

Children grow up among extended work-crews. Tools carry family histories, and a deliberately unrepairable device is considered an insult. Clans adopt outsiders who contribute to the convoy, although distrust runs deep where outsiders once owned the factories. Their songs record industrial accidents as readily as victories. Many Freeholders fear permanent settlements: a factory can become a prison again if its people forget how to leave.

## The rolling war

The army uses cheap rifle mobs to occupy space, maulers to open close terrain and powerful riders to turn an opening into a rout. Goblin scouts and artillery crews make this aggression possible. Their machines are robust, loud and repairable with workshop tools. Magical Smokebinders coordinate the emotional rhythm of a charge, while Rivet Doks keep momentum from ending at the first mechanical failure.

Rolling Thunder gives them extra striking power after movement, but their discipline deteriorates under concentrated suppression. They can deliver a tremendous first impact and then struggle to change the plan. Low-cost bodies do not mean unlimited food, and bulky engines consume fuel at an alarming rate. A skilled opponent interrupts the charge, separates the mechanics from the guns and forces the Freeholds to fight several small wars instead of one glorious advance.

## Freedom and appetite

Cinderjaw treaties are strongest when they specify a concrete exchange and weakest when they require obedience to an abstract hierarchy. The Hornbound Exodus trades route maps and livestock with them; the Kharad respect their workmanship more than their maintenance records. The Compact alternates between employing Freehold mercenaries and fighting clans that raid frontier stores.

The Dominion is an especially dangerous rival because both peoples salvage, improvise and learn from defeat. Cinderjaw leaders accuse the rats of replacing one factory master with a thousand secret masters. Dominion officers call the Freeholds strategically wasteful. Both are capable of exploiting the other’s weaknesses. Their conflict over foundry worlds often leaves the surviving civilian workshops richer in technical knowledge and poorer in everyone who knew how to use it safely.

## Cinderwake

A planet of disconnected furnace cities and free-running rail convoys. No ruler owns every track, and every map includes negotiated rights of passage.

## Nine-Sparks Drift

A goblin repair flotilla built around a shattered refinery. Its moving docks are the closest thing the Freeholds have to a neutral capital.

## Redwheel Reach

A frontier of fuel lakes and dry basins where rival clans test engines and settle disputes through endurance races as often as battle.

## Chainbreak Hosts

Liberation-minded clans free industrial labourers and absorb them into their convoys. They risk overextension by promising rescue wherever a furnace still has locked doors.

## Redwheel Brotherhoods

Cavalry and road-war clans believe mobility is the only secure form of sovereignty. They distrust fortifications and often underestimate patient siege armies.

## Nine-Sparks Leagues

Goblin-led technical federations build reliable artillery and communications. Their bargaining power grows whenever louder allies discover that engines require more than enthusiasm.

## Smoke-Crown Circles

Ritualists maintain clan memory and moderate destructive rivalry. Some preach restraint; others mistake a spectacular explosion for a divine instruction.

## Gorvakka Chainbreaker

Gorvakka broke the lock on the furnace complex where her clan had worked for generations, then refused its management throne. She became warboss by sharing the captured transport fleet. Her personal generosity coexists with impatience that can turn a negotiated campaign into a premature charge. Mikka is one of the few people permitted to tell her that courage cannot tow an empty fuel tank.

## Mikka Nine-Sparks

Mikka is a goblin engineer and elected convoy general, carrying nine ignition keys from nine campaigns. He remembers every promise made to his crews and presents invoices at politically inconvenient moments. His aim is a federation that can defend itself without depending on one charismatic warboss. Enemies who dismiss him as an assistant tend to discover their retreat route has already been dismantled.

## Urzha Smoke-Crowned

Urzha reads the residue of old fires and insists that the dead are witnesses, not fuel. She ended a clan feud by revealing that both sides had been supplied with sabotaged ammunition. Her magic can sustain an assault, but her political purpose is to make the Freeholds capable of stopping one. She fears that endless liberation wars could become an empire by another name.

## 0 AC — The First Broken Airlock

Abandoned forge workers seized the transports intended for their supervisors. The oldest share-fire laws date from the argument over who boarded first.

## 167 AC — The Nine Convoy Winter

Mikka’s technical league survived a blockade by converting war engines into heaters and freight haulers. The episode made logistics a recognised form of clan leadership.

## 306 AC — The Furnace That Answered

A stolen reactor began granting wishes in the voices of dead workers. Urzha exposed a Hollow Star pact inside its control systems and destroyed it despite the fuel crisis that followed.
