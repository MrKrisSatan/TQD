# Aurelian Compact

Hold the light. Command the storm.

An alliance of fortified city-states. Disciplined rifle formations, rail armour and Aether Cantors defend the surface against the empire below.

Original faction created for Ashcrown.

## The alliance that refused a crown

The Aurelian Compact began as the emergency government of seven human cities whose orbital supply network failed during the Crown fracture. Each possessed one indispensable resource: food, water, reactors, archives, shipyards, medicines or rail. None could survive the others’ destruction. Their first treaty was written on ammunition crates during a winter siege, and its most enduring clause forbade any city from monopolising the maintenance knowledge needed to keep another alive.

Over three centuries the seven-city pact became a federation of inhabited worlds, station republics and frontier duchies. It inherited the uniforms and cavalry traditions of several dead empires without accepting a single imperial bloodline. Its citizens disagree about almost everything except the memory of what isolation costs. Aurelian rhetoric celebrates the ordinary person carrying a public duty through extraordinary danger. Its darker reality is that wealthy cities can disguise their priorities as everyone’s emergency.

## Citizenship and command

The Assembly of Lamps apportions votes between population and essential contributions. An elected First Marshal receives operational control only for a declared emergency; renewal requires civilian assent. Provincial generals retain considerable freedom, creating a military that is adaptable but sometimes painfully slow to coordinate. The Civil Audit accompanies armies to investigate requisition, prisoner treatment and the temptation to extend an emergency indefinitely.

Service is an occupation rather than a hereditary caste. Soldiers return to trades, teach reservists and carry veterans’ voting rights back into local politics. Cavalry houses retain ceremonial titles but cannot exclude recruits by birth. Aether colleges are publicly chartered. Their arguments with intelligence officers over secrecy are constant, because a spell that powers a hospital can often be adapted into a weapon. The Compact’s greatest internal conflict is whether protection justifies temporary dictatorship, and how long temporary can honestly last.

## An army of connected arms

Lantern Fusiliers establish a reliable firing line while Wardens occupy dangerous ground. Hussars scout, screen and pursue; Cuirassier Exolancers wait for a decisive heavy charge. Railcrawlers engage enemy armour and Sunfall batteries dislodge entrenched formations. Cantors provide magical support without replacing the army’s logistical base. Engineers and railway troops are considered combat essentials, not background labour.

Interlocking Fire represents their common target language: a first volley identifies range and movement, allowing the next unit to shoot more accurately. The doctrine fails when officers cluster too closely or stop listening to reconnaissance. A Compact force should win by choosing where to combine ordinary tools. It should not possess the strongest version of every specialist weapon. Its common equipment simplifies repair, but its coalition supply schedules and dependence on roads create vulnerabilities a patient enemy can exploit.

## The frontier bargain

The Compact buys pressure machinery from the Kharad and grain from worlds protected by the Verdant Synod. Both relationships are uneasy. Industrial expansion sometimes destroys the very ecosystems needed to feed the cities, while a dwarf contract can bind a future government to a predecessor’s expensive promise. The Daughters are trusted as impartial rescuers but resist being treated as an auxiliary military branch.

The Dominion is feared because it can make a supposedly secure city dependent on hostile infrastructure without moving a visible border. The Compact therefore maintains independent water maps and rotating ammunition depots. Vesperglass agents exploit electoral scandals; the Hollow Star targets the exhausted citizens who believe public institutions have abandoned them. The Compact’s answer must be competent government as well as victory. A defended city that no longer trusts its defenders has already lost a different war.

## Aurel Gate

The assembly capital occupies a rebuilt orbital elevator. Its public archive contains the original seven-city treaty and every subsequent emergency renewal.

## Vesper Plain

A grain world of railway towns and cavalry schools. It is unrelated to the Vesperglass Cabals; the shared root comes from an older word for evening.

## Kestrel Foundry

Shipyards and steelworks circle a cold moon. Its unions supply both artillery crews and some of the Assembly’s most persistent critics.

## The Seven Lamps

The constitutional centre prizes combined-arms professionalism. Its officers fear the fragmentation of the alliance more than any single invading army.

## The Marcher Leagues

Frontier worlds favour scouts, light cavalry and local initiative. They resent delayed reinforcements and are vulnerable to charismatic would-be protectors.

## The Cuirassier Compact

Heavy cavalry houses converted inherited privileges into public service charters. Their rivalry concerns honour, funding and whose casualties politicians remember.

## The Civic Arsenal

Engineers, railway officers and gun schools argue that a well-supplied infantry company is worth more than a spectacular hero. They are frequently right and rarely popular.

## First Marshal Cassian Rook

Rook rose after refusing an order to destroy a city’s water plant to deny it to the enemy. His subsequent defence saved the plant and cost his regiment half its strength. He now chairs an alliance that admires his restraint while demanding cheaper victories. His command style coordinates fire, reserves and withdrawals; his private fear is becoming indispensable enough that the Assembly stops questioning him.

## General Maerin Holt

Holt was a railway surveyor before a raid stranded her work crews behind enemy lines. She organised an evacuation along an abandoned freight spur and later rebuilt it into an offensive supply route. Her campaigns turn on bridges, fuel and repair workshops. Rivals call her unimaginative until their brilliant advance runs out of ammunition beside a depot she built weeks earlier.

## Warden-General Selka Marr

Marr led the campaign to publish safe Aether practices after a military laboratory accident poisoned a civilian district. She is both an accomplished Cantor and a stubborn constitutionalist. On campaign she uses wards and precision magic to keep ordinary troops functional. She distrusts prophecy that cannot be cross-examined and has repeatedly challenged Lumen seers to disclose the alternatives they chose not to mention.

## 18 AC — The Winter of Seven Lamps

The founding cities pooled supplies during a siege and rejected the admiral who offered protection in exchange for hereditary rule. The treaty became the Compact’s foundation.

## 204 AC — The Iron Road War

Holt’s predecessors defeated a much larger raiding host by protecting junctions rather than every mile of rail. Modern mobile reserve doctrine dates from this campaign.

## 309 AC — The Buried Referendum

A city voted to leave the Compact just as Dominion tunnels appeared below its reservoirs. Rook protected its evacuation despite the vote, establishing that citizenship rights were not payment for political loyalty.
