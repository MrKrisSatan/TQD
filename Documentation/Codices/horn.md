# Hornbound Exodus

No fence shall name our children.

Beastfolk caravan nations combine centaur dragoons, armoured chariots and industrial minotaur shock troops.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## The nations that would not be fenced

The Hornbound Exodus is a confederation of beastfolk populations displaced by the Crown’s industrial expansion and later by its collapse. Goatfolk, centaurs, minotaurs and many smaller lineages were classified by former empires as labour categories before they were recognised as peoples. Some inhabited worlds for millennia; others were shaped by ancient gene-work whose records survive only as property inventories. The Exodus refuses to make that distinction a test of legitimacy.

Its founding migration began when several communities discovered that their supposedly temporary resettlement camps had become hereditary estates. They broke the perimeter fences, seized transport and carried their schools, elders and seed stocks into the open country. The road became a political institution: no person should lose their right to leave merely because a government finds their labour convenient. This ideal makes the Exodus a refuge, but also creates difficulty whenever a common decision requires a community to stay and fight.

## Hearths on the move

A caravan nation consists of hearth councils connected by route assemblies. A Road-Mother or Road-Father coordinates a migration season but does not own the herds, wagons or people travelling with it. Large communities include fixed farms and towns whose residents maintain membership through trade and the preservation of safe passage. Mobility is a right, not a command that everyone must remain nomadic.

Centaur populations often serve as dragoons and couriers, while minotaur work-clans specialise in heavy construction and assault. These are practical traditions rather than moral ranks. Storykeepers record roads as relationships: where water was freely shared, where a bridge was defended, where a host betrayed guests. The biggest internal divide lies between communities seeking recognised borders and those who believe borders inevitably become cages. Both can point to real histories that support their fear.

## Raiding as a means of survival

Hornbound armies move rapidly, strike logistics and avoid prolonged artillery duels. Rifle herds screen centaur dragoons; Stormcart Chariots provide mobile guns; minotaurs break positions that cannot be bypassed. Shamans draw on remembered migrations and the physical rhythm of collective movement. Their magic helps armies find routes, but does not create food or replacement axles.

Running War grants broad infantry mobility and powerful charges. Poor armour and short effective ranges make exposed advances costly. Raiding is economically necessary for some caravans and opportunistic predation for others; the Exodus contains both liberators and bandits. Its leaders must distinguish a legitimate seizure from an excuse to take a weaker settlement’s winter stores. If they cannot, the road becomes an empire that simply refuses to write down its borders.

## A place at the table without a throne

The Exodus shares routes and anti-slavery campaigns with the Cinderjaw Freeholds, but objects when their liberation columns consume more supplies than the freed people can replace. The Synod negotiates seasonal migration through living landscapes, sometimes banning a route to protect an endangered ecosystem. Compact frontier governments vary between welcoming skilled migrants and treating every caravan as an invasion.

The Dominion is especially resented for undermining water routes and turning refuge tunnels into military assets. The Riftbound Ascendancy recruits among displaced northerners, promising the power to destroy every wall. Hornbound elders warn that a pact can be a fence carried inside the body. Their conflict with the Ascendancy therefore concerns what freedom means after escape: the ability to move without permission, or the power to make everyone else move at your command.

## The Long Road

A chain of mapped migration corridors across several worlds, connected by leased transport gates. It is recognised as sovereign territory by some powers and as a legal impossibility by others.

## Nine-Hearth Basin

A neutral seasonal assembly ground with permanent hospitals and schools. Weapons may be carried, but disputes cannot interrupt access to water.

## Stormhoof Steppe

A high-wind world supporting centaur communities and enormous mobile agricultural platforms. Its roads are built to be repaired without central permission.

## The Open Hearth

Confederation loyalists defend migration rights and negotiated passage. Their armies favour balanced escorts because a victory is worthless if the civilian wagons are lost.

## Stormhoof Circles

Centaur military societies favour mounted fire and rapid pursuit. They resist being treated as transport assets rather than equal political communities.

## The Ironhorn Clans

Minotaur builders and breachers specialise in opening roads through hostile fortifications. Their reputation for strength conceals a sophisticated tradition of structural engineering.

## The Red Road

Predatory raiding factions claim every settled storehouse represents stolen freedom. The wider Exodus condemns their indiscriminate attacks but struggles to police groups that can simply leave.

## Road-Mother Ygra Manypaths

Ygra first led a migration after a government revoked her people’s right to use the river they had irrigated for generations. She negotiated passage through hostile territory by offering bridge-building skills instead of tribute. Her authority depends on maintaining that distinction between cooperation and submission. She is a patient coalition commander who becomes uncompromising when an ally proposes sacrificing the slowest wagons.

## Tarek Stormhoof

Tarek was offered a hereditary throne by a Compact client state that wanted his centaur followers to become permanent border cavalry. He refused and led them back onto the road. His dragoons combine accurate moving fire with sudden charges. He believes mobility should serve collective safety, but younger riders admire his victories more than his warnings against personal glory.

## Bhorun Gatebreaker

Bhorun learned demolition as a captive construction worker ordered to reinforce a prison wall. He later brought down that wall without collapsing the occupied cells behind it. His reputation is built on precise destruction rather than brute rage. In battle he leads minotaur assaults, while in council he asks who must live beside the ruins after the army has moved on.

## 54 AC — The Opening of the Camps

Several beastfolk communities coordinated an escape through industrial transport tunnels. Their temporary alliance became the first route assembly.

## 182 AC — The Bridge of Names

A caravan defended a bridge long enough for an enemy settlement to evacuate. The treaty that followed established the Open Hearth’s principle of reciprocal refuge.

## 308 AC — The Seventeen Fences

Bhorun’s forces dismantled a chain of fortified labour estates. Red Road raiders then looted the liberated stores, forcing Ygra to fight an internal campaign over the meaning of the victory.
