# Sauridian Helion Dynasty

We were entrusted with the dawn.

Reptilian city-temples field reliable cohorts, raptor cavalry and solar-armed megafauna guided by brood resonance.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## Custodians of an unfinished dawn

The Sauridian Helion Dynasty inhabits temple-cities built around ancient solar engines. Its reptilian peoples preserve biological and astronomical records older than any present human state, but age has not made those records complete. The Dynasty believes it was entrusted with maintaining a network that once regulated climates and star routes. Its oldest inscriptions describe a duty without clearly identifying the authority that assigned it.

For centuries the rulers interpreted stewardship as obedience to a sacred timetable. The Crown fracture made that certainty impossible. Some engines continued to operate while their appointed skies no longer matched the predictions. Others demanded actions that would harm the populations they were supposed to sustain. The modern Dynasty is divided between those who believe the custodians have misunderstood the instructions and those who believe the instructions themselves must now be changed.

## Broods and civic purpose

Sauridian society combines several reptilian lineages adapted to different environments and tasks. Broods are educational and kinship communities, not an absolute caste system. Small-bodied Glassfins often become scouts, broad Shellguards favour heavy work and defence, and crest-bearing cohorts supply much of the line army. Individuals can cross these traditions, though old temple houses do not always make it easy.

The Sun-Regent is chosen through a combination of brood councils, military evaluation and astromantic examination. Succession disputes arise when these methods disagree. Hatcheries are treated as civic institutions, and their protection is one of the few priorities capable of uniting rival temples immediately. The Dynasty’s long lives encourage careful planning, but can also make leaders underestimate the urgency felt by younger allies. A promise to return in forty years is not comforting to a settlement under attack today.

## Reliable strength, living engines

Sunscale infantry are durable and predictable, making the Dynasty a forgiving army to learn. Crestguard defend the command network; Glassfins scout; raptor riders supply a mobile assault arm. Solarback Behemoths and regulated salamander breeds carry biological weapons integrated with mechanical stabilisers. The animals are trained and cared for as valuable partners, although the degree of genuine respect varies between temple houses.

Brood Resonance reinforces infantry armour and objective control near a commander. It represents disciplined signalling and Aether-assisted coordination, not complete mind control. The army grows less efficient when spread too widely or when leaders are removed. Heavy beasts require food, veterinary care and suitable transport; solar systems need stored energy when local conditions are poor. Stable scaling comes from maintaining those supports, not from limitless monsters appearing without an ecological cost.

## The burden of inheritance

The Dynasty shares astronomical research with the Lumen Arc but dislikes decisions justified by futures that can be rewritten after the fact. It trades durable ceramics and climate machinery with the Compact and Kharad. Relations with the Synod are productive when stewardship is negotiated and hostile when a temple declares itself owner of a living world because an ancient engine stands there.

Verminhorde engineers covet the solar reactors and biological archives. The Dynasty fears theft not merely as lost equipment but as a misuse of a system whose larger purpose remains uncertain. The Riftbound want to transform the engines; the Hollow Star wants to make worship their fuel. Sauridian reformers argue that the custodians’ real task is to protect living people from inherited machinery when necessary. Traditionalists consider that argument the first step toward abandoning the only duty that gives their civilisation meaning.

## Helion Prime

The central temple-world contains the First Meridian, a solar engine that has never stopped running even though no one can fully explain what it powers.

## Xala’s Crown

A warm archipelago world whose brood cities integrate hatcheries, colleges and raptor reserves. Its civic architecture is deliberately accessible to several body plans.

## The Obsidian March

A volcanic frontier of ancient relay sites and armoured megafauna. Temple expeditions compete to interpret newly exposed inscriptions before rivals claim them.

## The First Meridian

Orthodox temples favour reliable cohorts, heavy guardians and strict command cohesion. Their strengths become liabilities when old procedures no longer fit new conditions.

## The Open Sun

Reformist brood councils advocate adapting the ancient duty to present lives. They field mixed forces and welcome technical exchanges with other peoples.

## The Raptor Courts

Mounted traditions specialise in reconnaissance and decisive charges. Their honour codes emphasise care for mounts, though competitive prestige can still encourage reckless losses.

## The Deep Observatory

Astromancers preserve long records and investigate the engines’ hidden connections. Their secrecy protects dangerous knowledge and sometimes prevents necessary correction.

## Sun-Regent Xalatek

Xalatek was selected after ordering a ceremonial engine shut down when its activation threatened a populated valley. The successful shutdown saved the valley and erased the only surviving record of the engine’s intended purpose. He now rules a people divided over whether he fulfilled or betrayed their inheritance. His battlefield leadership is steady, building strong formations around an intact command network.

## Crest-Marshal Issara Venn

Issara’s raptor formations combine inherited hunting signals with modern communications. She rose by defending a hatchery without waiting for a temple council to complete its jurisdictional debate. Her popularity among younger broods makes conservative rulers uneasy. She believes tradition earns respect by remaining useful and has no patience for commanders who treat avoidable casualties as proof of devotion.

## Star-Reader Oq-Telan

Oq-Telan discovered a persistent drift in the Dynasty’s sacred timetable. He initially concealed it while checking the instruments, then realised that several earlier readers had done the same. His present mission is to recover those suppressed observations. He commands powerful Aether disciplines but distrusts revelations that cannot be compared against physical evidence.

## Before 0 AC — The Custodial Ages

Surviving records establish solar maintenance traditions long before the fracture, but disagree about the identity of the original builders. The uncertainty is part of the setting, not a solved origin.

## 91 AC — The Silent Meridian

A temple followed an ancient activation schedule and found that its promised star route no longer existed. The loss created the first organised reform movement.

## 303 AC — The Hatchery War

Issara held a brood world against raiders while its government debated command authority. Her victory forced the Dynasty to recognise emergency action without hereditary permission.
