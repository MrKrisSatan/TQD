# Argent Testament

No life is beneath the oath.

Engineered expeditionary soldiers carrying legally binding memories of the people they failed to save. Expensive, durable formations win through overlapping fire and measured advances.

Original The Quiet Dread faction; broad archetype inspiration only.

## The volunteers who inherited a debt

The first Testament was a list of civilians stranded outside a sealed orbital shelter. Its military governor had followed every evacuation regulation and still abandoned sixty thousand people. The surviving rescue crews did not overthrow him. They placed his own orders beside the names of the dead and demanded a law under which obedience could no longer excuse abandonment. Their descendants now carry that law into battle. The Argent Testament recruits adults who have already served civilian rescue institutions. Augmentation buys the endurance needed to remain on a failing world after ordinary forces must depart; it is neither an inherited caste nor proof of moral superiority.

## The making of a witness

A candidate spends seven years as a witness before receiving the dense skeletal lattice and redundant circulation of a full soldier. Most candidates become medics, navigators or advocates instead. Each completed warrior accepts a testament: the recorded testimony of a person who died because a system failed. It must be recited accurately, including criticism of the military. These voices are private obligations rather than a collective consciousness. Some soldiers have discovered that their cherished testimonies were edited, and the resulting crisis of trust has become the order’s deepest internal conflict.

## Houses, courts and command

The Argent houses are mobile institutions: a rescue fleet, a regiment and a court travelling together. A commander can order a retreat, but must submit its reasoning to civilian witnesses afterwards. The First Listener coordinates campaigns without owning the houses. Their autonomy makes them unusually resistant to a single corrupt ruler and painfully slow when several legitimate obligations conflict. Officers wear blank ivory faceplates on duty so that testimony concerns the decision rather than the celebrity of the person making it. Names are spoken openly at the subsequent inquiry.

## Arms of the long vigil

Testament armour is a repairable pressure hull, plated with pale ceramic and supported by copper load frames. Coil weapons favour precise bursts over spectacular expenditure. Shield files establish safe corridors while breach cadres destroy strongpoints and heavy escorts carry civilians out through the breach. Choirwrights use Aether to hold failing structures together; the same geometry can pin an enemy formation. The Last Witness, a walking archive protected by overlapping shield vanes, anchors these operations. Its immense defensive field is strongest around nearby infantry, making separation a deliberate counter to an otherwise formidable battle line.

## Allies who do not agree

The Daughters of the Unbroken Dawn welcome the Testament’s refusal to abandon the vulnerable, but dispute its insistence that every revelation remain open to evidence. Compact governments value the houses until an inquiry reaches a politically useful officer. Vault auditors admire their contracts and distrust their willingness to incur impossible debts. The Oathscar Reclaimers claim to be the only honest heirs of the original rescue crews: they argue that the dead demand conquest rather than testimony. Argent commanders must resist turning a defence of the living into a war to silence that accusation.

## The quiet beyond the relays

Three houses vanished after entering a region where distress calls continued but human voices had ceased. The last surviving transmission contained a perfectly ordinary maintenance checklist spoken in the voices of several long-dead witnesses. The Testament calls this phenomenon the Quiet Dread. An investigation is underway, but commanders disagree over whether the silent worlds contain people awaiting rescue or a mechanism learning how to ask for it. The player’s expedition is authorised to recover evidence, protect living populations and refuse orders that cannot survive public accounting.

## Orison Anchorage

A ring of rescue docks around a cold ocean world. Its civilian courts meet in the same halls used to assemble expeditionary armour, so testimony is heard above the sound of repairs. No house may own a permanent seat.

## The White Causeway

A chain of pressure shelters spanning a shattered moon. Each shelter is named for the people who maintained it during the original evacuation. Training expeditions must cross the entire chain while repairing, rather than merely overcoming, its failures.

## Sable Hospice

A neutral station treating veterans from several hostile powers. Testament protection is conditional on disarmament within its hull. The station has become a fragile diplomatic channel precisely because the order refuses to make recovery depend on political obedience.

## House of the Open Door

Specialists in breached habitats and contested evacuations. Their commanders insist on identifying an exit route before approving an assault. Shield files, field restorers and the Last Witness protect the resulting corridor.

## House of Difficult Evidence

Investigators and reconnaissance troops who recover records from hostile territory. They are respected by civilians and disliked by officers whose reputations depend on convenient gaps in an archive.

## The Copper Vigil

Engine crews responsible for the order’s oldest armour. They argue that a repaired machine should retain visible evidence of its failures. Copper seams and exposed replacement plates are therefore marks of responsibility rather than shame.

## First Listener Cael Orison

A veteran rescue pilot elected after refusing a ceremonial triumph. Orison links widely separated units through careful fire discipline. He worries that his reputation for restraint now prevents subordinates from reporting situations that require violence.

## Breach-Marshal Iona Vey

An engineer who survived the collapse of a station she had certified safe. Vey leads from the breach, retaining every failed structural calculation as a lesson rather than destroying the evidence. Her assault cadre treats speed as a means of rescue, never an end in itself.

## Choirwright Sen Aravel

A former acoustic archivist able to hear the deformation of metal through Aether. Aravel believes the silent relay signals are altered testimony and insists on preserving them, despite pressure from officers who would destroy the transmitters.

## The Hearing at Meridian Nine

An Argent expedition discovers that a Compact governor fabricated a distress call to obtain military support. The governor’s enemies then attack real evacuation ships. Saving those ships while preserving evidence against their owner becomes the campaign’s central dilemma.

## The Last Unedited Voice

Oathscar notaries seize the only surviving original record of the corridor bargain. Three Argent houses disagree over whether rescuing the archive’s living custodians justifies sacrificing the document that might free millions from the Reclaimers’ debt claims.
