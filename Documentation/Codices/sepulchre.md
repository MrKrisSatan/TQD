# Sepulchral Continuum

Death does not annul a duty.

Undead citizens inhabit ossuary bodies and spectral engines, defended by mortuary law and necromantic networks.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## A republic after death

The Sepulchral Continuum formed when a failing colony discovered that its ancestor archive could inhabit artificial bodies through Aether resonance. The first revenants were not conquerors or aristocrats. They were engineers needed to repair systems no living person fully understood. Their return saved the colony and immediately created a constitutional crisis: did the dead possess authority, employment rights, family claims and a duty to leave when their work was finished?

The Continuance Charter answered with a principle still contested today. Identity may survive death, but consent must survive with it. A citizen can authorise a limited second service, decline resurrection, or demand final release. Enemies often see only skeleton troops and assume a mindless necromantic tyranny. Some Continuum officials encourage that fear. Others spend their entire second lives proving that a dead society need not become a society ruled forever by the dead.

## The living register

The government divides authority between living citizens, returned citizens and independent Notaries who verify identity and consent. Neither population can amend the Charter alone. A restored person must demonstrate continuity through private memories and chosen witnesses; imperfect reconstruction is treated as a new person with rights, not a defective copy to be discarded.

Bodies range from carefully preserved remains to ceramic ossuary frames and spectral projections supported by machines. Wealth still distorts access to desirable forms. Poor citizens may return in standard military chassis while the rich commission graceful replicas. Reformers seek equal restoration rights, but a universal promise would exceed the archive’s capacity. Families must negotiate grief rather than have it erased. A returned parent may be legally the same person while having missed decades that cannot be repaired by a new body.

## The army that keeps its appointments

Testament Cohorts and Sarcophagus Guard absorb losses that would exhaust ordinary troops. Dreadwake Knights provide mounted counterattacks; artillery carries destructive resonance in shells patterned after funerary bells. Notaries restore damaged formations by reconnecting their bodies to intact identity records. Suppression cannot reduce their action allowance, although disrupted senses can still impair accuracy.

Continuance is maintenance, not limitless regeneration. The game restores small amounts of health only to surviving infantry near a caster; destroyed formations do not simply stand up again. In the lore, a body can be rebuilt only if its archive and consent record remain intact. Severing local necromantic links, isolating Notaries or destroying storage infrastructure can make losses permanent. The army is also relatively slow, and its conservative command culture sometimes mistakes endurance for an answer to manoeuvre.

## The right to an ending

The Continuum trades legal expertise and ancient technical knowledge with the Compact and Kharad. Both appreciate its reliability while fearing contracts that might never expire. Its relations with the Synod are complicated: some groves welcome the respectful preservation of memory, while others see revenant bodies as life withheld from natural cycles.

Vesperglass memory slavery and Hollow Star soul consumption are fundamental enemies of the Charter. Against them the Continuum fights to protect personhood rather than merely territory. Verminhorde memory harvesting is regarded as theft from the unconsenting dead, though Dominion officials argue that discarded neural traces cannot own anything. The Continuum’s own danger is quieter: officials can indefinitely postpone release by declaring another emergency. Its integrity depends on remaining willing to honour the final wish of citizens whose continued service would be very useful.

## Morrowrest

The founding colony surrounds a vast public archive. Gardens above the storage vaults are maintained jointly by living and returned families.

## The Quiet Parliament

A station whose chambers alternate between warm living quarters and resonance halls. Every session ends by reading the names of citizens granted final release.

## Ossuary Reach

A frontier of industrial tomb-cities repeatedly attacked for their rare memory substrates. Its defence forces contain many of the Continuum’s oldest volunteers.

## The Testament Legions

Charter loyalists emphasise controlled restoration and disciplined line warfare. They refuse to deploy a citizen whose service consent cannot be verified.

## The Eternal Custodians

Traditionalists believe survival requires longer terms of posthumous service. Their critics fear that emergency duty is becoming ownership.

## The Last Door

Notaries dedicated to release rights specialise in destroying hostile soul machinery. They are prepared to dismantle their own state if it becomes a prison.

## The Unremembered

New persons formed from incomplete archives seek recognition independent of the dead they resemble. Their innovative methods challenge the assumption that age creates authority.

## First Executor Nemea Sorn

Nemea wrote the legal ruling that prevented a restored governor from reclaiming office automatically. She later died defending the archive containing that ruling and returned for a limited military term. Her authority rests on disciplined adherence to limits she could easily evade. She has published the date on which she intends to leave command permanently, forcing her government to prepare for a future without her.

## Grave-Marshal Othric Vale

Othric has led cavalry through four reconstructed bodies, each with subtly different instincts. He keeps the old armour beside his quarters to remember that continuity requires work. His doctrine uses endurance to hold an enemy in place before a decisive mounted strike. He fears losing his capacity to grieve more than losing another body.

## High Notary Iseth Marr

Iseth is alive and has explicitly declined future restoration. That choice makes her a trusted guardian of the release process and a target for those who think continuity should be compulsory. She maintains emergency keys capable of shutting down compromised archive networks. In battle she restores troops carefully, refusing shortcuts that might return someone different merely because the army needs another rifle.

## 22 AC — The First Return

Dead engineers restored a failing colony’s thermal systems. The resulting argument over their rights produced the earliest Continuance Charter.

## 139 AC — The Succession of Empty Chairs

Returned governors attempted to resume office across six worlds. Nemea’s ruling and a short civil war established that memory of power did not confer current authority.

## 311 AC — The Last Door Campaign

Notaries destroyed a Hollow Star soul refinery and released its prisoners rather than recruit them. The military cost was high; the precedent was deliberately absolute.
