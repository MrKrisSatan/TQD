# Convent of the Hollow Star

What heaven withholds, we shall take.

An all-female demonic order whose oath-engines steal grace, consume souls and make devotion into an instrument of dominion.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## The miracle that became a debt

The Convent of the Hollow Star is an all-female corrupted military and demonic order founded by Vaelora Nox, once a senior protector within the Unbroken Dawn. During a catastrophic siege she concluded that people saved by the sisters owed more than gratitude. They owed obedience sufficient to prevent another disaster. When the Unbroken Light would not answer a ritual designed to bind that obedience, she judged the refusal a defect in heaven rather than in the demand.

Vaelora built the First Hollow Engine from stolen relay components and confiscated devotional records. It produced a convincing imitation of grace by consuming the freedom of the people whose vows powered it. Her followers called the result proof that divinity could be improved. The Daughters called it enslavement. The schism was not caused by women becoming ambitious or by faith inevitably becoming evil. It was caused by a deliberate decision to treat other lives as material for power.

## A sisterhood of possession

Every member of the Convent’s military and governing order is a woman. Its soldiers, generals, priestesses, pilots, gunners, engineers and named commanders are female. Its sovereign daemon-saints originate from women or manifest as female beings within its theology; its war engines are crewed or ritually directed by women. It does not conceal male equivalents inside renamed infantry units. Captive populations and external clients may include anyone, but they are not the order itself.

Recruitment mixes genuine ideological conversion, opportunism and coercive dependence. Some women join because they believe protection must be enforced; others seek power after abandonment, revenge against a specific institution, or freedom that the Convent promises and then mortgages. Their motives explain them without excusing what they do. Rank is measured by the vows a sister controls and the power she can extract from them. Affection can exist between individuals, but the state continually turns intimacy and trust into instruments of leverage.

## The theology of the hollow

The Convent teaches that the universe recognises only successful claims. A blessing is a resource, a soul is a reservoir of possible futures, and mercy is merely the stage before a beneficiary understands the price. Its rituals invert the Dawn’s protections: confession becomes reconnaissance, sanctuary becomes dependency, and an oath freely given is rewritten to survive the giver’s refusal. The Hollow Star is the name for the absence left when a sacred form remains after its benevolent purpose has been removed.

Its miracles are real in effect and corrupt in source. Soulfire can mend the caster by harming another; possessed armour can preserve a soldier while gradually changing what she wants preserved. Demonic patrons compete to inhabit the order’s oath-engines. Vaelora refuses to acknowledge any patron as a rightful master, while building a system that cannot operate without their hunger. The Convent’s most closely guarded secret is how much of its apparent sovereignty is already collateral.

## The army of stolen grace

Hollow-Vow Sisters form a disciplined armoured core. Thorned Halo elites assault weakened positions; Veil Confessors sabotage trust and identify targets; Reliquary Sisters endure inside possessed exo-armour. Mourningstar Cavaliers and the Cathedral of Want give the order a distinct mounted and heavy demonic arm. Its guns, incense projectors and artillery are maintained by trained women artificers, so the army remains dangerous even when a ritual fails.

Stolen Benediction rewards enemy destruction with Aether, while Soulfire turns offensive magic into limited self-healing. The faction is encouraged to sustain pressure and finish vulnerable targets. It cannot heal without cost or resurrect everything it loses. Strong armour makes units expensive; disruption of casters and concentrated long-range fire can deny the close exchanges it wants. Its demonic appearance should communicate ritual authority, predation and damaged sanctity through armour, masks and architecture, not rely on sexualised uniforms.

## The empire inside a promise

The Convent seeks worlds dependent on protection, then offers it on terms designed never to end. It prefers an exhausted ally to an empty ruin because a functioning society supplies more vows. This distinguishes it from the Riftbound, who seek transformation for its own sovereignty, and from Vesperglass, who commodify information without necessarily claiming the whole soul.

The Dawn remains the Convent’s principal ideological enemy because it demonstrates that protection need not create ownership. The Continuum attacks its soul machinery; the Synod resists compulsory communion; the Compact struggles against its influence among abandoned citizens. The Dominion may trade prisoners or technology with it in the new setting, but no lasting trust is possible between an empire that harvests useful memory and an order that insists the entire person is already pledged. The Convent can conquer a great deal. Its system makes freely given loyalty increasingly difficult to recognise when it finally appears.

## Nox Vespera

A fortress-world built around the First Hollow Engine. Its black chapels imitate places of refuge while routing every oath toward central reservoirs.

## The Cathedral Drift

A fleet of mobile convent-fortresses that offers military aid to collapsing governments. The landing of its relief ships is often celebrated before anyone reads the terms.

## The Unsainted Choir

A breached relay complex where female daemon-saints acquire stable form. Each manifestation changes the architecture to reflect what its patron believes devotion should cost.

## The Thorned Halo

Vaelora’s ruling convent favours elite infantry, public ritual and tightly controlled political dependence. It insists the other orders exist only because it permits them to.

## The Iron Lament

Sabeth’s professional war-sisters emphasise armour, guns and reliable logistics. They resent ecstatic waste but remain willing participants in the order’s coercive empire.

## The Velvet Confession

Infiltrator-priestesses acquire secrets, recruit compromised officials and prepare populations for dependency. Their power depends on keeping some promises while concealing the price of others.

## The Unsainted Choir

Daemon-saints and their female attendants seek deeper transformation. They increasingly question why a formerly mortal abbess should command beings she claims to have liberated from human limits.

## The Black Reliquary

Fleshsmiths and possessed veterans preserve damaged sisters inside ritual armour. Their workshops are places of remarkable technical care and systematic spiritual captivity.

## Abbess-Maledict Vaelora Nox

Vaelora still speaks in the language of responsibility. She can describe every disaster her authoritarian system claims to prevent and almost none of the lives it has made impossible to escape. Her power comes from controlling the infrastructure of stolen grace, not merely from personal sorcery. She wants the Daughters to acknowledge that her methods work, because their refusal exposes the moral choice she insists was only practical necessity.

## War-Prioress Sabeth Vane

Sabeth is a disciplined woman general who dislikes waste, theatrical punishment and uncontrolled mutation. None of that makes her benevolent. She believes an efficient tyranny is preferable to a disorderly one and measures mercy by whether it improves compliance. Her armour hosts are the Convent’s most reliable military instrument. She is preparing contingencies for the day Vaelora’s demonic creditors demand more than the state can pay.

## Morwenna, the Unsainted

Morwenna entered the Convent seeking the power to protect her settlement and rose by sacrificing the futures of people who trusted her. Her transformation into a sovereign female daemon-saint made that pattern permanent: she can preserve what she possesses, but struggles to imagine love without possession. She now challenges Vaelora’s authority while offering followers an even more intimate form of captivity. Her Soulfire sustains her by consuming the possibilities of the wounded.

## 198 AC — The First Hollow Engine

Vaelora’s forced miracle severed her faction from the Dawn. The survivors chose to preserve the engine rather than free the lives bound inside it.

## 257 AC — The Mercy of Nox Vespera

A threatened world accepted permanent military protection. Its government survived, but every subsequent attempt to end the treaty was interpreted as sacrilege.

## 311 AC — The Last Door Defeat

Continuum forces destroyed a soul refinery and released its captives. Sabeth withdrew in good order while Vaelora publicly denied that anything valuable had been lost.

## 312 AC — The Unsainted Succession

Morwenna’s growing influence divides the Convent between human command and daemon sovereignty. The crisis is unresolved and supplies several possible campaign directions.
