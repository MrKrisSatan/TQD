# Vesperglass Cabals

Every certainty has a price.

Exiled elven dynasties raid for living memories, weaponising stolen futures and precise terror.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## Exiles from an edited history

The Vesperglass Cabals began among elven houses accused of hoarding safe routes during the fracture. Their defence was simple: the Principalities had also chosen whom to save, but enjoyed better control over the surviving archives. Some accusations against the exiles were true. Others were politically useful inventions. The cabals built their identity around refusing any moral authority that could not survive possession of the complete evidence.

Their tragedy is that they answered selective history by making history a private commodity. Vesperglass memory-vaults can store sensory experience, emotional associations and fragments of unrealised possibility. A memory can be sold, pledged, stolen or used as collateral. The richest cabals own evidence that can unmake governments. They claim to expose hypocrisy while building a society in which most people cannot afford the truth about themselves.

## The market in identity

A cabal is simultaneously a household, intelligence service, creditor and armed corporation. Its Archon controls a vault whose contents make subordinates useful and vulnerable. Contracts may pledge years of service, a treasured recollection or the right to be remembered by one’s descendants. Some retainers enter willingly to escape poverty; others inherit obligations their ancestors could not repay.

Ordinary life still contains affection, craft and dissent. Glassworkers make exquisite instruments, families preserve illegal copies of their own histories, and underground mutual-aid circles help people recover names held as debt. Cabal rulers tolerate limited resistance because a completely desperate population produces poor intelligence. This calculation reveals the faction’s central cruelty: freedom is treated as a negotiable allowance from those who can confiscate the evidence that a person ever possessed it.

## Predation as a science

Vesperglass forces are mobile, precise and fragile. Retainers pin targets with accurate fire while raiders isolate officers, healers and artillery crews. Their predictive systems retain the native elven reroll, but their doctrine adds particular force against already wounded formations. They prefer to finish a compromised target rather than trade evenly with a fresh one.

The army’s equipment is expensive and difficult to maintain outside cabal workshops. Needle accelerators and phase blades excel against valuable targets; they struggle to justify their cost against endless expendable bodies. Heavy attrition exposes the small population beneath the theatrical confidence. Raiding commanders must also protect the portable vaults that contain their political authority. Destroying a tank is an inconvenience. Freeing the memories it carries can end a dynasty.

## The price of knowing

The cabals trade intelligence with almost anyone and trust almost no one. Their rivalry with the Principalities is a struggle over the meaning of the shared past as much as territory. They consider the Verminhorde Feast of Memory crude but strategically alarming: the rats might obtain by mass ritual what Vesperglass monopolises through costly infrastructure.

The Hollow Star is a tempting customer and a dangerous competitor. Cabals sell memories; the Convent claims ownership of the soul that remembers. The Continuum treats involuntary memory extraction as an assault on civic personhood, making their wars unusually ideological. The Daughters maintain shelters where escaped debtors are allowed to give testimony without selling it. Cabal propaganda calls those shelters theft. Their rulers fear them precisely because that claim sounds less convincing when spoken aloud.

## Vesperglass

A duskward habitat ring with mirrored courts above heavily shielded vaults. Its public plazas are designed so everyone can be watched from somewhere beautiful.

## The Silent Exchange

A mobile market whose location is communicated through encrypted fragments of song. Buyers may conceal their faces but must declare what they are willing to lose.

## Nacre Hollow

A captured moon used to grow neural crystal. Its workers preserve a clandestine archive of everything the official vault says never happened.

## The Crownless Vault

Nysera’s oldest cabal specialises in political leverage and precision troops. It claims neutrality while ensuring every negotiated peace leaves someone in its debt.

## The Quiet Debt

Assassin syndicates prefer a few irreversible acts to prolonged warfare. Their internal honour concerns exact contracts, not the innocence of the target.

## The Unlived Bank

Oracle houses trade predictive possibilities and finance other cabals’ campaigns. Their accounts include obligations that will mature only if a particular future occurs.

## The Broken Mirror

Insurgent retainers seek to abolish hereditary memory debt. They still use raiding tactics, and freedom from the cabals does not automatically make them merciful conquerors.

## Archoness Nysera Vhal

Nysera’s birth name disappeared during a vault fire that killed all seven people entitled to inherit ahead of her. She neither confirms nor denies responsibility. Her long reign depends on balancing clients who each believe they possess a secret capable of removing her. On the battlefield she coordinates accurate fire and exploits wounded enemies; politically she prefers debts that can be collected repeatedly to victories that end a relationship.

## Vael Corven, the Quiet Debt

Vael once discovered that the memory of his childhood had been assembled from several unrelated debtors. He now collects proof against the cabal that made him, while remaining one of its most profitable commanders. His raids target evidence stores and command structures. He is dangerous because he genuinely wants freedom and has never accepted that other people might deserve it without first becoming useful to him.

## Seer-Matron Eshara Vey

Eshara lends unrealised futures to clients facing impossible choices. The interest is paid in possibilities their descendants will never receive. She sees herself as a practical banker in a universe of scarcity, and dislikes crude cruelty because it damages collateral. Her magical support makes small forces extraordinarily efficient, but every apparent rescue creates an obligation she intends to enforce.

## 41 AC — The Exile of the Mirror Houses

A dispute over evacuation archives became armed secession. The departing houses took navigation instruments and the memories of thousands who had not consented to leave.

## 188 AC — The War Without Birthdays

A cabal attempted to erase a rival’s lineage from every vault. Surviving families now celebrate the day their identities were recovered rather than the dates of their births.

## 307 AC — The Shelter of Unpaid Names

Dawn sisters defended a refugee station containing escaped memory debtors. The cabals lost the battle and an expensive propaganda campaign insisting the refugees were stolen property.
