# Oathscar Reclaimers

The debt outlives the debtor.

Veteran rescue legions who turned impossible obligations into predatory supernatural contracts. Armoured shock troops, bound engines and debt sorcerers thrive in violent close battles.

Original The Quiet Dread faction; broad archetype inspiration only.

## The unpayable rescue

The Reclaimers descend from an expedition that remained inside a collapsing relay corridor to evacuate a chain of mining worlds. Their command petitioned for relief until its reactors failed. Something beyond the corridor answered with power, and the expedition survived by promising to return every life it had saved. The officers believed the language symbolic. When evacuees began vanishing from their new homes, they learned that the promise had acquired a creditor. Instead of repudiating the bargain, the surviving command declared that it would conquer enough lives to pay the debt.

## An empire of substitutions

Oathscar holdings operate through substitution: another worker serves your term, another household pays your levy, another world answers your distress call. Powerful debt houses accumulate layers of proxies until no one can identify the original obligation. The population is not uniformly fanatical. Some inhabitants believe that punctual payment protects them from worse horrors; others hide fugitives or sabotage the ledgers. Reclaimer officers call these acts selfish because an unpaid debt falls on someone else. Their entire moral system makes solidarity appear dangerous while presenting organised cruelty as responsible administration.

## The creditor without a face

The entity called the Unwritten Creditor has never issued a clear command. Its agents appear as gaps in records, shadows wearing the posture of absent people and signatures that move between documents. Different warbands disagree over its intentions. The Notaries believe it measures suffering, the Red Executors believe it rewards successful conquest, and the Empty Choir believes that fulfilling every debt will erase the universe. These beliefs produce very different armies, but each finds convenient evidence in victories bought at other people’s expense.

## The surviving legions

Reclaimer armour retains the layered engineering of the rescue era, beneath violet seal wax and iron contract plates. Their ammunition carries fragments of binding formulae that tear the firer as well as the target. Veteran files deliberately close the distance so that stolen vitality can repair their wounds. Bound engines are inhabited by entire defaulting households, their conflicting voices channelled into propulsion. The Covenant Eater is the largest of these engines: a titan whose attack consumes its own armour to project a devastating blood contract. It can be worn down by denying it kills and forcing it to cross open ground.

## The right to refuse

The Argent Testament preserves copies of the expedition’s original relief requests. Those documents prove both the abandonment and the absence of consent from the rescued populations. This makes the Testament a threat beyond its military strength. Hollow Star convents offer the Reclaimers rituals that cancel individual obligations by sacrificing communities; some debt houses accept, while others consider it an unlawful distortion of their bargain. The Verminhorde trades recovered identities to Oathscar notaries but never grants them access to the All-Mother’s genealogical archives.

## A schism waiting for a voice

A growing movement of scarred infantry has begun writing the word Refused across contract plates. They do not deny what happened in the corridor. They reject the claim that survival created the right to own everyone else. No rebellion has yet endured, because liberated settlements often remain dependent on the same engines that enforce the debts. A Reclaimer campaign therefore contains a persistent contradiction: its strongest victories extend a system that even many of its own soldiers secretly want to end.

## The Court of Arrears

A city built around a rescue ship that never completed its final docking sequence. Its population lives in compartments labelled with the names of creditors rather than streets. Public clocks count obligations instead of hours.

## Vowfall

A world of contract foundries where armour plates are stamped with inherited debts. The foundries employ living workers alongside bound engines containing the voices of former owners. Production interruptions are treated as moral failures.

## The Unregistered March

A belt of settlements deliberately omitted from official ledgers. Refugees try to survive without entering any bargain the Creditor might enforce. Reclaimer officers deny these settlements exist while repeatedly requisitioning troops to destroy them.

## The Red Executors

Assault cadres who believe a debt becomes legitimate through the strength required to collect it. Their officers encourage close combat because stolen vitality immediately repairs the wounds inflicted by their own weapons.

## The Margin Notaries

Sorcerers who alter the interpretation of existing bargains rather than inventing new ones. A missing comma, an erased witness or an ambiguous name may be enough to bind a living formation to an engine.

## The Refusal Cells

Underground groups within veteran regiments who preserve evidence of coerced contracts. They remain enemies on the battlefield, but their existence offers a story route toward revolt rather than treating every Reclaimer as incapable of choice.

## Executor Mael Rusk

The last surviving officer to sign the corridor bargain. Rusk remembers the exact number of people saved and cannot bear to admit that his effort to honour them has become a greater disaster. His orders drive relentless close-range fire.

## Lady Nerezza Vow-Eater

A duellist who purchased her first command by taking the debts of an entire city. Each victory adds another name to her armour. She regards compassion as a weakness in the wording of a contract and seeks opponents whose defeat will prove it.

## Notary Ossian Null

A sorcerer whose body was erased from every official register. Null speaks through a mask suspended above an empty pressure suit and uses stolen signatures to turn battlefield casualties into regenerative power.

## The City That Paid Twice

A liberated city presents proof that its debt was settled generations earlier. Two Reclaimer houses each insist that the payment belongs to the other’s account. Their rivalry turns into a siege in which the population is punished for possessing accurate records.

## The Unwritten Campaign

Ossian Null follows a sequence of victories absent from every military archive. The missing campaigns appear to have created obligations that even the Creditor’s most faithful servants cannot explain. Null intends to own that uncertainty before anyone can resolve it.
