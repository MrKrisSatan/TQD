# Kharad Vault Concord

The mountain remembers every promise.

Dwarf pressure-forges field armoured infantry, gravitic artillery and walking bastions, with no mounted arm.

Original Ashcrown faction.

## Civilisation under pressure

The Kharad Vault Concord was born in sealed dwarf habitats where the difference between politics and engineering was often a door that would not close. Their bodies adapted to high gravity and restricted environments, but their culture was shaped more profoundly by interdependence. Air had to be accounted for; structural warnings had to be believed; an expert could not safely be silenced because a powerful family disliked the calculation.

Before the fracture, the Vaults maintained deep Crown foundations for rulers who rarely visited them. When those rulers disappeared, the dwarfs inherited debts, dangerous machinery and millions of people trapped behind failing seals. The Concord joined independent holds through a common emergency code. Its founding oath does not demand that a hold never fall. It demands that those who know it is failing tell the truth while evacuation is still possible.

## The government of recorded promises

Each hold elects a Castellan and a civic pressure council. Guilds certify competence, but certification can be challenged before travelling rune judges. The Nine Seals govern air, water, heat, labour, defence, inheritance, asylum, debt and emergency command. A promise is recorded with its conditions and witnesses so that later rulers cannot conveniently remember only the flattering parts.

This system preserves institutional memory and produces formidable bureaucracy. Old contracts sometimes outlive the circumstances that made them reasonable. Reformers argue that the purpose of an oath is protection, while traditionalists fear that reinterpretation turns every obligation into a suggestion. Families adopt apprentices across bloodlines, and a master’s reputation includes the independence of those apprentices. The most shameful guild failure is not making a mistake; it is concealing one until someone else dies from it.

## The slow wall of iron

Kharad infantry carry heavy plate, precise mass-drivers and portable seal shields. There is no conventional cavalry arm. Short-ranged boreframes and walking bastions provide the counterattack that mounted troops supply elsewhere. Artillery uses gravity manipulation to punish fixed positions, and rune surveyors reinforce the geometry of a defended line. A planned withdrawal is drilled as carefully as an advance because the Concord has buried enough brave fools behind doors they refused to abandon.

Anchored Plate makes stationary infantry particularly difficult to shift. Movement exposes joints and disrupts overlapping shield patterns, so the army must decide when ground is worth leaving. It suffers on widely separated objectives and against opponents who refuse a decisive engagement. Heavy ammunition, replacement seals and specialist maintenance are expensive. An invulnerable-looking line can become helpless if its transports are cut off or its commanders defend a strategically irrelevant fortress out of pride.

## Memory without petrification

The Vaults trade with the Compact and Sauridian Dynasty, argue with the Synod over mineral extraction and compete bitterly with Dominion tunnellers. They distinguish a legal salvage claim from theft even when the stolen machine is already a wreck. Cinderjaw engineers earn grudging admiration for practical invention; Freehold warbosses earn less admiration when they treat an invoice as a challenge to single combat.

The Sepulchral Continuum unsettles Kharad law because it asks whether a dead signatory can still amend a contract. Rune judges are divided. The Lumen Principalities are considered unreliable negotiators when they invoke futures that witnesses cannot examine. The Concord’s long-term danger is becoming a perfect archive of obligations that no living society can fulfil. Its best generals defend the people who make promises, not merely the inscriptions left behind.

## Kharad Nhal

A high-gravity world honeycombed with civic vaults. Its largest assembly hall is built beside a deliberately exposed pressure gauge.

## The Nine-Seal Ring

An orbital habitat chain housing neutral courts, arbitration foundries and refugee docks. Weapons are checked alongside commercial accounts.

## Flintwake Deeps

A contested mining frontier whose ores can stabilise Aether reactors. Its seismic maps are among the most frequently stolen documents in the setting.

## Holds of the First Seal

Traditional defensive armies favour Hearthguard and prepared gun lines. Their strength is discipline; their weakness is treating inherited boundaries as sacred strategy.

## The Open Ledger

Reformist guilds prioritise mobile repair, evacuation planning and transparent command. Their opponents accuse them of turning every oath into a committee discussion.

## Deepmeasure Circuits

Rune judges and gravitic engineers unite science with binding magic. They refuse to certify a weapon whose failure modes its sponsors will not disclose.

## The Unhoused Companies

Dwarfs whose holds were destroyed serve as expeditionary troops. They are determined to earn new homes without repeating the mistakes that cost the old ones.

## High Castellan Dorrik Vane

Dorrik ordered the evacuation of his ancestral hold six hours before its central shield failed. He saved its population and lost a family tomb containing nine centuries of records. Traditionalists call him the Castellan Who Left; survivors elected him Keeper of the Nine Seals. He commands slowly because he wants every officer to understand the withdrawal plan before anyone fires.

## Maela Flintledger

Maela proved that an ammunition guild had altered maintenance records to conceal a lethal defect. Her testimony destroyed a powerful patron and made her an unlikely military hero. She commands engineers with unusual autonomy and can hold an army together after its armour should have failed. Her ambition is a Concord where admitting uncertainty is recognised as professional strength.

## Orrun Deepmeasure

Orrun is a rune judge who served inside the failing Crown foundations during a century-long maintenance mission. He trusts measurements, but has learned that a frightened commander can distort their interpretation without falsifying a number. His battlefield magic stabilises forces at critical moments. He is investigating whether some of the Crown’s oldest failure warnings were deliberately ignored.

## 7 AC — The Closing of the Nine Seals

Independent holds adopted common emergency law after a rescue mission failed because three incompatible authorities each claimed final command.

## 221 AC — The Airless Siege

Kharad troops defeated an invading fleet by evacuating outer habitats and fighting from sealed internal fortresses. The victory created their modern layered defence doctrine.

## 300 AC — The Flintledger Hearings

Public exposure of fraudulent shell casings nearly split the Concord. Maela’s reforms survived because front-line companies refused to load unverified ammunition.
